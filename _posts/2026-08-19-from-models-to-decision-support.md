My work sits at the intersection of operations research, supply chain analytics, and applied AI. Across research and industry, I keep returning to the same question: **how can an analytical model improve a real decision?**

## A research foundation

My doctoral research at the National University of Singapore focused on operations management methods including queueing theory, stochastic processes, dynamic programming, discrete-event simulation, and location-allocation optimization. These methods provide a rigorous language for representing uncertainty, congestion, resource constraints, and trade-offs.

My research projects have included emergency-service location-allocation and predictive maintenance for manufacturing systems. Although the applications differ, both require translating a complex operating environment into a model that supports a defensible decision.

## Moving into practice

In industry, the model is only one part of the solution. A useful decision-support system must also reflect business rules, expose assumptions, support scenario comparison, and communicate results clearly to cross-functional stakeholders.

That is the perspective I bring to my current work in supply chain planning and operational improvement: start with the decision, choose the right level of analytical detail, and build a workflow that people can actually use.

## What I plan to write about

This site will collect practical notes on optimization modeling, supply chain analytics, simulation, and the design of decision-support tools. I will focus on reusable methods and lessons while keeping company data and confidential implementation details private.
